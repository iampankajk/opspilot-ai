# OpsPilot AI — Current-State Incident Workflow

## Purpose and status

This document records the assumed current-state workflow for investigating a fictional SaaS company's APAC payment incident. It is a discovery artifact: it describes the company, users, systems, evidence, responsibilities, decisions, and follow-up as they exist in the scenario.

The workflow is a set of working assumptions for the portfolio scenario, not findings from interviews with a real company or validation of a real operating process. It does not define OpsPilot capabilities, requirements, architecture, or an MVP feature set. Those decisions remain for later discovery and design.

## Scenario

At 10:30 AM, Grafana Alerting detects an unusual increase in payment failures and sends an alert through PagerDuty. The alert reports a 12.4% failure rate against an expected rate below 2% in the last five minutes, affecting Payment Processing in APAC. Around the same time, Support has received about 15 reports of failed payments and three reports of possible duplicate charges. The alert confirms elevated failures; it does not confirm duplicate charges or their cause.

**Fictional tabletop exercise reference-time decision:** Incident reference date is 20 October 2026. The reference timezone is Asia/Singapore (Singapore Time, SGT, UTC+08:00). The alert time is 10:30 AM SGT (`2026-10-20T02:30:00Z`); its five-minute measurement window is 10:25–10:30 AM SGT (`2026-10-20T02:25Z` to `2026-10-20T02:30Z`, minute precision). This is an explicit exercise-design choice, not a date or timezone inferred from the incident ID or APAC geography. Timestamped synthetic machine-readable records will use ISO 8601 UTC; facilitator materials display SGT. Preserve source timezone and precision when applicable, distinguish event time from ingestion/creation time, and do not invent missing timestamps or precision.

The investigation treats elevated payment failures and possible duplicate captures as related but independent workstreams until evidence establishes a connection.

## Current-state investigation journey

### 1. Detect, notify, acknowledge, and assess

1. Grafana Alerting detects the five-minute payment failure rate breach and sends the alert to PagerDuty.
2. PagerDuty notifies the on-call Payments Operations Engineer. The engineer acknowledges/takes ownership or escalates to another responder.
3. The engineer reviews the alert's metric, threshold, time window, APAC region, affected service, trigger time, and dashboard/runbook links.
4. The Incident Commander / Payments Lead owns severity and investigation prioritization. The current example starts at SEV-2 (High); the team may reassess severity as impact becomes clearer.

### 2. Establish scope and collect customer symptoms

5. The engineer opens Grafana and checks overall health, baseline comparisons, volume, failure categories, geography, provider, payment method, error codes, latency, timeouts, and retries. They compare recent and longer time windows to estimate when the issue began and whether it is worsening.
6. **Proposed, unvalidated Support-side identification and escalation workflow:** Support checks the Zendesk requester profile, ticket channel/authentication, and available identifiers; email alone is not definitive payment identity. Support searches customer/order records through an authorized read-only lookup interface using verified customer/account references. If the order remains unidentified, Support asks for non-sensitive information (order number/confirmation, approximate payment time, amount/currency, payment method type, and relevant context), never full card numbers, CVVs, passwords, or payment credentials. Support attempts correlation, but leaves multiple possible matches unresolved. If no reliable match is established, Support escalates the original Zendesk ticket to Payments Operations. A complete first-handoff packet contains the Zendesk ticket ID and issue; available customer/account references; amount/currency/approximate time/payment method where available; source/provenance for each identifier; lookup attempts; systems and search criteria; outcomes/candidate matches; explicitly missing information; whether customer information was requested; and a clear reason for escalation. A complete packet describes what Support investigated and what remains unresolved; it does not necessarily identify the payment. The engineer reviews Zendesk escalations; support reports are evidence of symptoms, not proof of cause or duplicate charge.

   **Proposed, unvalidated workflow assumption for this pain-point exercise:** for 15 illustrative complaints (8 payment failures, 4 timeouts, 3 possible duplicate charges), the engineer first groups similar reports by available symptom, time, region, provider, payment method, order, and error attributes; investigates representative tickets to find common patterns; and individually investigates exceptions and possible duplicate charges.

   **Proposed, unvalidated grouping guardrails:** complaints are grouped only when they plausibly share an incident, service, and time window; potential financial harm and significant customer impact receive priority; symptom/error/time/region/provider/method similarity informs grouping; materially different patterns are split; and possible duplicate charges or unusual financial outcomes receive individual financial assessment. Sampling can identify a common pattern but cannot establish every customer's outcome. The engineer preserves ticket references, grouping rationale, selected representative cases, and unresolved cases, and escalates when impact may be high, evidence conflicts, or scope is unclear.

   **Proposed, unvalidated decisions left to engineering judgment:** the engineer chooses grouping dimensions, whether complaints belong together, which and how many representative tickets to inspect, whether more sampling is needed, when to split or combine groups as evidence changes, and which other cases need individual investigation. Each ticket retains an outcome: individually matched; associated with a broader incident pattern but not individually verified; unmatched for insufficient information; or requiring individual financial investigation. Individual financial verification remains necessary for every possible duplicate-charge complaint.

7. **Proposed, unvalidated documentation process:** Slack is used for real-time coordination and PagerDuty remains the authoritative incident record. The Payments Operations Engineer promptly records material findings in PagerDuty, including evidence supporting or contradicting a hypothesis, possible duplicate captures, significant scope/severity changes, confirmed customer/financial impact, remediation recommendations/approvals, and findings that change the next investigation step. Entries include occurrence time, evidence, classification (observation/hypothesis/confirmed), relevant transaction/trace/provider/deployment references, next step, and owner. Routine observations (for example, repeated transactions with the same error or additional supporting log references) may be grouped into a later update if evidence, timestamps, and decisions are preserved. Slack discussions link to the related PagerDuty entry where practical; not every Slack message is copied. Before handoff, responders reconcile material findings, decisions, approvals, remediation actions, and open tasks against PagerDuty. The Incident Commander is accountable for record completeness; a designated scribe may help during busy incidents; incoming responders review the timeline and surface gaps.

### 3. Inspect individual payment attempts and provider state

8. From an aggregate pattern, the engineer searches the Payment Operations Console for affected transactions using time, transaction/order, region, provider, method, status, and available customer/account references.

   **Proposed, unvalidated incomplete-handoff behavior:** Payments Operations first reviews the escalation packet and searches the Payment Operations Console using the available identifiers; urgent/high-risk investigation is not paused solely to obtain a more complete handoff. The engineer identifies missing information and unclear Support lookup history, asks Support for prior search criteria/systems/results when needed, compares Support's work with Operations' results, and notes duplicated work, additional matches, and unresolved ambiguities. For example, a complaint with only email, ₹5,000, and an approximate payment time may yield three possible attempts; Operations starts from those details and asks Support whether account/order history was already checked. The engineer continues to Stripe/Grafana as needed. Repeating a search because of missing handoff context is distinguished from necessary independent verification of a high-risk financial outcome.

   Proposed measures include the percentage of incomplete packets requiring repeat searches, Operations active time spent repeating Support searches, clarification requests, Support response wait time, additional matches discovered, cases unresolved after repeat searches, and whether repeat work was necessary for independent financial verification or caused by missing handoff information.

9. For a possible duplicate charge, the engineer compares internal authorization, capture, settlement, reversal, refund, retry, and idempotency records with Stripe using provider references/Payment Intent IDs, order IDs, amount, currency, timestamps, and event IDs.
10. The investigation distinguishes:
   - Two attempts with one successful capture: not evidence of a duplicate capture.
   - Two distinct successful captures for the same intended order and amount/currency: strong evidence of potential duplicate capture; check reversals, refunds, and settlement.
   - Two authorizations with one capture: not evidence of two captured payments; pending authorizations may be temporary holds.
   - Repeated delivery of one webhook event: not evidence of multiple payments; verify underlying provider objects and distinct capture IDs.
11. Two captures support a duplicate-capture finding. Confirming two posted customer debits may require additional provider or issuer-side evidence. Each customer complaint receives a correlation confidence: high (stable matching identifiers plus corroborating attributes), medium (several attributes but no stable identifier), or insufficient (ambiguous/incomplete). Ambiguous matches remain unresolved; email alone is not enough.

### 4. Examine technical failure evidence and recent changes

12. The engineer uses identifiers from the console and provider records to search Loki logs and Tempo traces in Grafana Explore. They inspect the failure sequence and request path and compare behavior by version, region, service, and time.
13. The engineer compares the first abnormal behavior with relevant GitHub commits/PRs, GitHub Actions deployments, application versions, regions/environments, code/configuration differences, and runtime configuration audit logs. They check whether the affected scope aligns and whether different versions behave differently. Temporal correlation alone does not establish causation.
   **Proposed, unvalidated deployment and version-metadata assumptions:** standard rolling deployment, not a dedicated canary. In the illustration, deployment starts 10:12 AM; v2.8.0 and v2.8.1 overlap from about 10:15–10:18 while traffic shifts; deployment completes at 10:20 with only v2.8.1 remaining; the alert arrives at 10:30. At investigation time, concurrent version comparison may no longer be available. The designated deployment record/release ledger is authoritative for verified versions actually deployed to each production environment and region; GitHub Actions is execution evidence, GitHub is source-change history, and configuration audit logs cover runtime/out-of-band changes. The ledger is proposed to retain successful, failed, and rollback outcomes with links to the relevant workflow run and previous version. Historical Prometheus series may be associated with versions using a dedicated build-information metric and instance/deployment metadata; Loki logs expose `application_version`; Tempo traces expose `service.version`. Direct version metadata is distinguished from attribution inferred through deployment/instance history. Historical evidence remains subject to separate retention: 30 days proposed for Prometheus and Loki, and 14 days for Tempo. An incident older than 14 days may retain metrics and logs while traces have expired, leaving incomplete trace evidence; investigations within 14 days may have all three telemetry sources available. Brief rollout overlap is not a controlled experiment; historical comparisons must account for traffic, region, provider, payment method, and operating conditions. Do not assume a canary deployment is currently used.
14. Payments Service, Release/DevOps, and SRE/Infrastructure engineers help explain or verify changes and technical behavior as needed.

### 5. Form, prioritize, and test hypotheses

15. The team considers separate hypotheses for elevated failures and duplicate financial impact. Candidate failure hypotheses include provider degradation, APAC connectivity, a payment service deployment/configuration regression, and retry/idempotency issues. Candidate duplicate-related hypotheses include retry/idempotency defects, duplicate webhook processing, and repeated customer submissions.
16. The Incident Commander / Payments Lead prioritizes work using customer/financial impact, scope, urgency, evidence strength, and investigation feasibility. A potentially serious risk is not ignored solely because evidence is incomplete. The engineer leads technical investigation; Support contributes customer context.
17. The team labels each item as supporting evidence, contradicting evidence, or unverified. Root-cause confidence is classified as confirmed, likely, or inconclusive. Confirmation requires symptom consistency, direct technical evidence, temporal and scope alignment, sufficient examination of alternatives, and validation such as a controlled test, rollback, configuration correction, or recovery observation. Primary causes and contributing factors are recorded separately.

### 6. Coordinate, recommend, and control remediation

18. Findings and material decisions are shared in the incident Slack channel and copied to the PagerDuty record. The record distinguishes observations, hypotheses, and confirmed findings and preserves investigation timestamps, references, evidence, and actions.
19. The Payments Operations Engineer recommends remediation options and evaluates effectiveness, impact, risk/blast radius, reversibility, time, evidence strength, and rollback plan. Containment (reduce ongoing impact), remediation (correct the cause), and recovery (restore and verify normal service) are distinct activities.
20. The Incident Commander / Payments Lead decides which remediation proceeds. Sensitive or consequential operations require explicit authorization under company policy. Low-risk actions may follow previously approved runbooks and delegated permissions. The responsible engineering team executes approved technical remediation and records it in PagerDuty. **Proposed, unvalidated production rollback approval checklist:** provide (1) incident context/impact (ID, severity, service/region, start time, failure rate vs baseline, customer/transaction impact, whether ongoing); (2) deployment/technical evidence (current and previous known-good versions, deployment time, commit/PR, changes, metrics/logs/traces, version concentration); (3) rationale (expected effect, root-cause confidence, alternatives and why rollback is preferred); (4) risk (in-progress requests, schema/version compatibility, retry/idempotency, inconsistent states, other workflows, blast radius); (5) execution plan (target, owner, procedure, preconditions, duration, verification); (6) recovery/contingency plan (recovery metrics, lifecycle checks, observation period, failed-rollback and worsening-incident response); and (7) explicit request (exact action, approve/reject decision, execution owner, urgency-based deadline, additional approvers). A rollback may be proposed for emergency containment with root cause unconfirmed if evidence and action safety are stated separately. Proposed outcomes are Approved, Rejected (reason recorded and alternatives evaluated), or More information required (engineer supplies missing evidence/risk assessment). An emergency escalation path for unavailable approvers and its delegated-authority limits remain undefined and require validation.
21. The team does not blindly retry an uncertain payment, directly manipulate payment records without authorization, or issue a refund without the required approval. Examples of possible technical actions, subject to evidence and policy, include approved provider routing/failover, rollback, correcting unsafe retries, restoring idempotent webhook handling, or controlled request deduplication.

### 7. Verify service recovery and close the operational incident

22. After remediation, the engineer presents recovery evidence; action completion alone is not proof of recovery. The team checks payment success/failure rates and volume against agreed baselines (the scenario's expected failure rate is below 2%), provider and regional health, latency/timeouts/retries, lifecycle consistency, and customer impact.
23. The proposed fictional-company policy requires two consecutive 15-minute healthy windows (30 minutes total), with sufficient transaction volume, no recurring timeout spikes, normalized provider latency/retries, no unexpected duplicate captures, and uncertain outcomes reconciled. Longer observation may be needed for low-volume or high-risk cases.
24. The Incident Commander reviews the evidence and decides service recovery and operational incident closure. Service recovered, customer-impact remediation, and incident closed are separate statuses. Operational closure may occur while refund or customer follow-up remains open, provided those cases continue under their own owners and tracking.

### 8. Verify and manage customer financial remediation

25. For each suspected duplicate, the Payments Operations Engineer confirms two distinct successful captures for the same intended purchase, checks reversals/refunds/settlement, collects provider evidence, and documents whether impact is confirmed, not confirmed, or inconclusive. A duplicate-capture conclusion does not by itself prove two posted customer debits.
26. The Payments Lead and Finance representative determine the remedy based on payment state, provider evidence, impact, and policy. A captured duplicate may call for refunding the additional capture; an outstanding authorization may call for a void/release rather than a refund.
27. An authorized Finance Approver approves financial actions according to limits; high-value/exceptional remedies require additional approval. Authorized Payments Operations Staff execute an approved operation through the Payment Operations Console/provider integration and record capture reference, amount/currency, reason, approver, executor, provider refund reference, timestamp, and status.
28. A refund is not complete merely because it was submitted. Its status is one of:
   - **Pending:** The provider accepted the request but has not confirmed completion. Payments Operations monitors it and, if it exceeds the provider-specific expected window, escalates it to the Payments Operations Lead and Finance.
   - **Failed:** The provider explicitly rejected or failed the request. The team investigates the reason, verifies the original capture and remaining refundable amount, and determines whether an alternative remedy is needed. Any further financial operation requires appropriate authorization.
   - **Unknown:** The request timed out or its response was lost, so the provider-side outcome is unresolved. Payments Operations searches the refund reference and provider dashboard/API records and reconciles status; staff must not blindly submit another refund.
   - **Succeeded:** The provider confirms successful refund processing. This confirms provider processing, not necessarily that the customer's bank has posted the credit.

   Systemic refund failures affecting multiple customers are escalated to the Payments Lead / Incident Commander, and the underlying engineering issue is tracked separately in Jira.
29. A Finance / Payments Remediation Owner remains accountable for each customer financial-remediation case through verified financial outcome and completed customer follow-up. The Payment Operations Console tracks refund case ID, capture/order/customer references, amount/currency, provider refund ID, request/response times, status, last verification, owner, next follow-up, approval/execution history, and Zendesk ticket. Important status transitions are auditable.
30. The Support Engineer prepares customer communication from verified information; the Support Lead reviews/approves it under policy; the message is sent through Zendesk and associated with the original ticket. PagerDuty contains the incident-level customer impact/remediation summary. Jira tracks systemic engineering work.
31. **Refund execution case closure:** the Finance / Payments Remediation Owner reviews the case and records the final closure decision only when all of the following are satisfied:
   - The provider confirms successful refund processing.
   - Internal and provider records reconcile for the original capture ID, refund ID, amount, and currency.
   - Authorization, executing user, timestamps, and status history are recorded.
   - The customer has been informed of the verified refund outcome through Zendesk.
   - No unresolved refund exceptions or financial discrepancies remain.
   - If the bank has not yet reflected the refund, the expected posting timeframe has been communicated and any needed follow-up is separately tracked.
   - The assigned Finance / Payments Remediation Owner reviews the case and records the closure decision.

   Provider-confirmed refund success does not necessarily mean the customer's bank has posted the credit. If provider processing is confirmed and the above case-closure conditions are met, the refund execution case may be completed while a separate customer/bank-posting follow-up remains open and tracked. The Remediation Owner remains accountable for the overall customer financial-remediation case until that follow-up is complete; the refund execution case and the follow-up have distinct statuses.

### 9. Review, document, and improve

32. After operational closure, the Incident Commander / Payments Lead facilitates a blameless Post-Incident Review (PIR), with participants selected by incident scope: Operations, Payment Service Engineering, Support, SRE/Infrastructure, and Finance/Payments as relevant.
33. Proposed fictional-company schedule: preserve timeline/evidence during and immediately after the incident; prepare initial documentation within one business day; hold review within three business days; finalize the Confluence report within five business days; track follow-up actions to completion.
34. The PIR covers incident and customer/financial impact, timeline, root cause and evidence, response/recovery effectiveness, what worked or failed, and preventive/corrective actions. Unresolved hypotheses remain marked unresolved even after closure.
35. Jira actions have a clear description, priority, owner, due date, acceptance criteria, and link to the Confluence incident report. Examples include improving timeout monitoring, idempotency, reconciliation, or runbook guidance.
36. Runbooks, troubleshooting guides, incident reports, Jira tasks, and Git-managed scripts/configuration remain distinct. A proposed runbook update is tracked in Jira, drafted by an engineer, reviewed for technical accuracy by the relevant technical owner, approved by the Payments Operations Lead, and published with revision history. Metadata includes document ID/version, owner, reviewer, approver, updated/reviewed/next-review dates, applicable service/environment, and status (Draft, In Review, Approved, Deprecated). Responders use the latest applicable Approved version. A 90-day review cycle is proposed, with earlier review after material service/configuration/operational change. Historical incident reports are preserved.

## Evidence and decision guardrails

- Metrics describe aggregate behavior; logs record events; traces show a request path and where time or failure occurred.
- Alerting establishes an anomaly, not its cause. Aggregate metrics do not establish duplicate charges.
- Attempts, authorizations, captures, settlements, refunds, reversals, and webhook deliveries are distinct payment facts.
- Customer reports are evidence of symptoms; correlation to a payment has an explicit confidence level.
- Findings distinguish observation, hypothesis, and confirmation, and retain contradicting evidence and uncertainty.
- Failure root cause and duplicate financial impact are investigated independently unless evidence connects them.
- Remediation priority does not grant authority to execute. Sensitive operational, financial, and external communication actions follow approval policy.
- Technical recovery, operational incident closure, refund execution case closure, and customer/bank-posting follow-up have separate completion states and owners.
- Sensitive payment credentials and raw card data are not exposed in the investigation workflow.

## Open discovery questions and validation limits

This is internally consistent as a fictional operating model, but it is not validated by real user research. Before converting it into a problem statement or requirements, discovery should still test:

- Whether an on-call Payments Operations Engineer is the best primary user and whether this problem is frequent and costly enough to prioritize.
- Actual time spent, delay points, handoff failures, error rates, and current workarounds across the mapped systems.
- Whether complaint grouping is a documented process or responder judgment; validate using complaint/group counts, tickets selected for deeper investigation, individually matched/unmatched tickets, possible matches per search, active lookup time, repeat lookups/rework, and time from ticket creation to reliable match.
- Whether grouping judgment varies materially by responder; whether regrouping/rework or additional sampling delays investigation; and whether representative sampling ever misses a customer-level issue. Compare independent groupings and selections on the same anonymized ticket set, then review actual incident timelines and later-confirmed outcomes while accounting for incident context.
- **Proposed validation priorities, not findings:** (1) critical—missed customer-level financial issues; every possible duplicate-charge complaint requires an individual financial assessment; (2) high—materially inconsistent conclusions from the same evidence; (3) medium—additional active or elapsed time caused by repeat lookup, rework, or grouping; (4) context-dependent—different representative case selections, which are not inherently problematic if required high-risk cases receive individual verification and selection is explainable. Test with the same anonymized complaints and evidence for multiple responders, compare groupings/selections/coverage/conclusions/missed verified issues/time/rework, and have an independent reviewer establish outcomes from underlying payment and provider records. Investigation safety and conclusion quality take priority over efficiency.
- **Leading pain-point hypothesis, not a finding:** interpreting and correlating evidence across systems may be harder than locating it, with specialist dependency potentially adding waiting time. Measure evidence discovery (active search time, systems, repeated searches/rework), interpretation (active hypothesis-testing time, competing hypotheses, conflicting/insufficient evidence, conclusion changes, and comparison with an independently verified reference), and specialist dependency (waiting time and handoffs) separately. A fixed evidence-package exercise can isolate interpretation from retrieval; a separate end-to-end exercise can measure retrieval. Agreement between responders alone is not proof of correctness.
- **Proposed documentation validation measures, not findings:** measure time between a material Slack finding and its PagerDuty entry, the percentage of material findings recorded promptly or in a later batch, material findings absent from PagerDuty, contradictory/outdated entries, active documentation time, and handoff confusion attributable to missing information. For batched updates, record the stated reason (documentation interrupts investigation, observations need further correlation, or team habit). Compare across incidents to determine whether maintaining separate coordination and authoritative-record channels creates measurable overhead, delay, or inconsistency.
- **Leading batching-reason hypothesis, not a finding:** the engineer most often waits to correlate several observations before documenting a consolidated update; interruption from documentation is a secondary hypothesis, and established team habit is an alternative. For each batch, record the stated reason, number of observations, time from first observation to PagerDuty update, active documentation time, whether a material finding/decision was delayed, and whether handoff information was missing or contradictory. Interview engineers about their reasons rather than inferring them only from timestamps.
- **Working identifier-completeness hypothesis, not measured data:** in the illustrative 15 complaints, assume 9 (60%) include at least one stable identifier (Order ID or Customer ID) and 6 (40%) contain only approximate payment details. An Order ID is a stronger link to a specific order; a Customer ID may map to multiple orders/attempts; email can help find an account but is not definitive; approximate time/amount/currency can narrow matches but may leave multiple candidates. Validate separately the percentage with Order ID, Customer ID, both, or neither; candidate records per ticket; active time to reliable match; repeat searches/rework; unresolved rate; and how missing identifiers are eventually obtained.
- **Proposed, unvalidated intake assumptions:** an authenticated portal may populate Customer ID through a trusted integration when resolvable; Order ID is optional but requested; email is not treated as a verified Customer ID; amount/currency/approximate time are requested; method and transaction ID are optional; tickets remain submittable without stable IDs. Record identifier provenance (authenticated integration, customer, Support, or another internal system). To distinguish customer omission, intake design, and integration gaps, validate field presence and provenance against intake channel and source; measure identifier coverage for authenticated versus other channels and inspect cases where expected portal-derived Customer IDs are absent.
- **Proposed channel/identifier validation measures, not findings:** compare Ticket Channel, exact Intake Source, authentication status, Customer ID and Order ID availability/provenance, reliable-match rate, candidate payment records per ticket, active/elapsed matching time, repeat searches/rework, unresolved outcomes, and Support's manual identifier retrieval by channel/authentication cohort. This can test whether authenticated portal tickets have better Customer ID coverage, whether email/phone tickets more often lack stable identifiers, and whether missing identifiers correspond to slower or less reliable matching; association alone would not establish the cause.
- **Proposed Support identification/escalation validation measures, not findings:** measure tickets requiring customer follow-up, customer-response waiting time, active Support lookup time, identifier recovery rate, ambiguous matches, escalation rate, completeness of provenance/search history/missing-information notes at escalation, repeated Support or Operations lookups, and time from ticket creation/escalation to reliable payment correlation. Keep customer wait, Support active work, and Operations active work separate to distinguish customer omission, form limitations, integration gaps, and internal lookup friction.
- **Working first-handoff completeness hypothesis, not measured data:** among the six illustrative complaints without stable identifiers, assume three (50%) receive a complete escalation packet on first handoff; for the other three, lookup history, identifier provenance, or missing-information notes may be incomplete. Potential causes to distinguish are searches not consistently documented, Support lacking needed access, and variation in Support escalation practices. Validate percentage complete and missing fields by category, Operations follow-up requests, repeated Operations searches, active rework time, and time from initial escalation to a usable packet. Packet completeness is distinct from payment identification.
- **Working repeated-search hypothesis, not measured data:** of the three illustrative incomplete first handoffs, assume two may cause Payments Operations to repeat a search Support already performed. Count as potentially avoidable duplication only when the same search is repeated because prior search history/results were not documented. Keep this separate from necessary independent verification or an extended investigation to verify payment/provider records or potential financial harm. Validate incomplete handoff count, repeated Support searches, necessary independent verification, active time on avoidable repetition, additional information found, and delays caused by missing search history. The remaining incomplete handoff may reflect limited Support access, missing customer information, or an unsuccessful lookup.
- **Provisional approval-delay frequency hypothesis, not an observed finding:** material approval delays occur in some incidents, particularly for production payment-service changes, provider-routing changes, infrastructure failovers, and customer refunds; low-risk delegated or pre-approved actions may not require the same approval path. Illustrative scenario only: a rollback recommendation is complete at 10:45 AM, approval arrives at 11:15 AM, and the 30-minute gap may extend active payment failures.
- **Proposed approval-wait impact measures, not findings:** use incremental customer impact during the approval wait. Primary measure: additional unique customers experiencing payment failures. Supporting measure: additional failed payment attempts, distinguishing repeat attempts by already-affected customers from newly affected customers where possible. Financial-safety measures: additional confirmed duplicate captures, value still unreversed/unrefunded, and duration unresolved; a capture is exposure, not necessarily permanent loss if subsequently reversed/refunded. In the illustrative 10:45–11:15 window, compare these changes and assess whether the proposed rollback could reasonably have prevented some impact. Incremental impact during the wait alone does not show causation; action capability, timing, and incident evidence must support the relationship.
- **Proposed causal-validation criteria for rollback impact, not findings:** before approval, examine (1) version-specific behavior under comparable conditions (for example, failures concentrated in v2.8.1 while v2.8.0 remains healthy); (2) relevant code/configuration differences and a plausible mechanism (for example, a timeout changed from 30 to 5 seconds); (3) Loki/Tempo evidence for failure start, handling version, provider reachability, timeout location, and retries; (4) alignment between rollback target and affected version/service/region; and (5) rollback safety (previous version, schema compatibility, in-progress requests, retry/idempotency, blast radius, execution/contingency). Before approval this evidence supports a prediction, not proof. After rollback, validate the result using success/failure rates, provider timeouts, latency, retries, and transaction consistency, comparing suitable unaffected versions/regions or historical baselines where possible. Estimate potentially avoidable impact against a suitable reference adjusted for traffic, provider, payment method, and other relevant differences. Separate unique customers from attempts and timeouts from confirmed payment failures; a timed-out request may have succeeded at the provider. Do not attribute all approval-window impact to approval delay or claim rollback would certainly have prevented it.
- **Proposed delay interval measures, not findings:** separate (1) complete recommendation to approval decision, (2) decision to execution start, (3) execution start to completion, and (4) execution completion to verified recovery, to locate delay in approval, execution coordination, or recovery.
- **Proposed approval-delay validation measures, not findings:** record recommendation-preparation completion, request submission, approver receipt, decision, requests for additional information, grant/rejection, execution start, and execution completion. Measure time from complete recommendation to decision, approver wait, active review, clarification time, approval-to-execution time, total recommendation-to-execution time, unavailable-approver escalations, and whether customer/financial impact continued. Segment by action type, urgency/severity, approval level, approver availability, pre-approved-runbook status, and completeness at submission. Approval safeguards are not inherently inefficiencies; assess avoidable delay without weakening authorization, financial controls, or operational safety.
- **Leading approval-delay cause hypothesis, not a finding:** incomplete remediation recommendations are the most likely cause, followed by unclear approval authority; approver unavailability is an alternative, while careful risk assessment may be legitimate review time. For a rollback request, potentially missing details include supporting evidence, affected customer/transaction scope, rollback risk, last stable version, procedure, and effects on in-progress payment requests. Validate the share of requests requiring more information, missing categories, clarification cycles, wait and active review time, time preparing additional evidence, unclear ownership, and whether the decision changes after clarification.
- **Baseline rollback-request format hypothesis, not an observed finding:** before any standardized checklist, engineers may submit informal approval recommendations through PagerDuty or Slack, with links to dashboards, logs, and deployment history. The initial request may state the incident/abnormal behavior, suspected deployment/configuration change, rollback rationale, target version, and approval request, but presentation may vary. Potentially absent details include customer/financial impact, evidence strength, suspected-vs-confirmed cause, code/configuration differences, compatibility/safety, in-progress payment effects, execution owner/procedure, recovery criteria, failed-rollback contingency, and approval dependencies. Illustrative wording: “Payment failures increased after v2.8.1 was deployed. Grafana shows increased APAC timeouts, and Loki shows provider request failures. I suspect the deployment introduced a timeout regression and recommend rolling back to v2.8.0. Can we proceed?” This is a scenario hypothesis; not every engineer is assumed to submit an incomplete request.
- **Proposed rollback-request validation checklist, not an established policy:** compare first-submission requests with the seven sections recorded in the rollback approval process (incident impact, deployment/evidence, rationale, risk, execution plan, recovery/contingency, explicit decision request). Measure first-submission completeness, clarification requests/review cycles, missing categories, approver wait, active risk-review time, rejection reasons, and time from complete recommendation to execution. Distinguish missing information from legitimate risk review. Validate approval decision outcomes (Approved, Rejected, More information required) and the emergency escalation/delegated-authority policy before treating them as current practice.
- **Historical validation approach, proposed and subject to access/retention:** retrieve anonymized rollback discussions from Slack, which is the source for the original request, author/time, clarification questions and responses, evidence, approval discussion, edits, and thread replies where retained and accessible. Compare with the PagerDuty incident ID, official timeline, recorded recommendation/summary, decision and decision owner, notes, status changes, and action timestamps. Do not assume PagerDuty contains the complete Slack discussion. Reconstruct in order: original request; clarification(s); additional evidence; final recommendation; approval/rejection; execution start. For each event capture timestamp, author, content, source, incident ID, and whether it requests clarification or adds information. Measure initial request-to-decision elapsed time, clarification-cycle count, missing-information types, clarification-to-response time, complete-recommendation-to-decision time, decision changes after new information, and likely delay cause (incomplete information, approver availability, unclear authority, or legitimate risk review). Message timestamps show elapsed time, not active review time; measure active review separately through interviews or contemporaneous time records. Do not infer that incompleteness caused delay without linking it to clarification cycles/time or corroborating responder interviews.
- **Proposed, unvalidated specialist-dependency assumptions:** the On-call Payments Operations Engineer can retrieve and correlate most routinely available evidence independently. Payments Engineering is consulted for retry/idempotency implementation, webhook handling, complex traces/state transitions, code/configuration interpretation, and code-level remediation safety. SRE/Infrastructure is consulted for regional networking, DNS/TLS, Kubernetes/infrastructure health, database saturation, resource exhaustion, failover, infrastructure changes, and telemetry gaps. Internal provider-integration or external provider specialists are consulted when standard dashboards/APIs do not explain provider behavior or confirm payment, settlement, webhook, account-configuration, rate-limit, or refund states. Specialist help is not inherently a process failure; it may be necessary for safe or accurate investigation.
- **Current specialist-frequency hypothesis, not an observed rate:** specialist help is needed in some incidents, not every SEV-2 payment incident; it is more likely for regional connectivity, retry/idempotency behavior, unclear duplicate captures or settlement, or unexplained provider behavior. At least one handoff is considered reasonably likely in the illustrative APAC scenario because technical and financial questions remain unresolved. Validate by measuring the percentage of incidents with a handoff, handoff count per incident, wait time, specialist teams involved, conclusion changes, and rework after specialist input.
- **Leading specialist-outcome hypothesis, not a finding:** specialist input more often confirms or refines the Operations Engineer's leading hypothesis than completely replaces it, although a material change could prevent an incorrect root-cause assessment. For each handoff, record the pre-handoff hypothesis/confidence, request, specialist response/evidence, outcome (confirmed, refined, contradicted, or replaced), final independently verified conclusion, wait time, and time to verified conclusion. Compare rates of confirmation/refinement, material changes, newly introduced causes, prevented incorrect conclusions, and time from handoff to verified conclusion.
- **Proposed, unvalidated specialist handoff context:** include incident ID, affected service/region, time window, application version, transaction/order/provider references, trace/request IDs, observed behavior, evidence already collected, and the specific question. Measure specialist handoffs, wait time, active specialist investigation time, request type, repeat handoffs from incomplete context, post-response rework, and whether specialist input changes the conclusion.
- Test whether a bottleneck is primarily evidence discovery, interpretation/correlation, or specialist dependency before treating any as a significant product problem.
- Whether the proposed evidence, confidence, severity, recovery, and approval criteria match company policy and provider-specific realities; provider-specific refund processing windows also need validation.
- Which sources are available to an investigator under their actual access permissions and how identifiers are linked across systems.
- Whether the scenario should remain one incident with two parallel workstreams or be modeled as related incident and customer-remediation cases.
- Historical evidence availability still needs validation: whether Prometheus build-info/instance metadata reliably maps series to versions; whether ingestion and disk sizing allow the proposed 30-day metric window before a size limit purges data; whether proposed Loki 30-day/Tempo 14-day policies are configured and accessible; and whether significant-incident Confluence postmortems preserve the proposed evidence. Beyond 14 days, traces may be absent while metrics/logs remain. For PagerDuty, validate the proposed 12-month incident-timeline requirement against contract/account settings separately from 12-month configuration-audit record availability; check whether Audit API/Audit Trail Reporting is included in the account plan and accessible. For Slack, validate whether the paid plan and workspace settings preserve the proposed 12-month original-message, thread, edit-history, and deletion-history fields; latest visible content alone is insufficient for historical reconstruction. For deployments, validate that the authoritative deployment ledger is independently retrievable and preserves metadata for the proposed 12 months. The proposed GitHub Actions workflow-log/run/check/status retention is 90 days; validate effective account/org/repository settings and access. The fictional-company artifact policy proposes 90 days for routine Actions artifacts and 12 months for selected production release packages in a logically separate durable release/package repository or object-storage location, with no provider selected. Validate actual per-artifact and run-deletion behavior (including whether deleting a workflow run deletes associated artifacts), the shared configurable retention setting, plan/organization/enterprise limits, cost, access controls, security, and whether the selected release can be reconstructed from its retained digest/provenance. Do not treat a workflow-run reference as proof that expired logs or artifacts remain available; changing retention does not restore expired records. Git commits/PR availability and runtime configuration audit-history retention remain separate validation/open questions. Further gaps: Stripe dashboard/API accessibility and retention for payment, capture, refund, settlement, request, and webhook records. Determine whether anonymized linked evidence and independent outcomes can be assembled for validation. Any future controlled/staged rollout evaluation remains an open question, not current capability or an OpsPilot architecture decision.
